    Excited States via IH-FSMR-CCSD¶

    An alternative approach for decoupling the singles excitation space from the space of double and higher excitations is to use the so called Fock space multi-reference coupled cluster (FSMRCC) method. The method is similar to STEOM-CCSD, but much more flexible in terms of formulation.

    General Description¶

    FSMRCC is originally based on an effective Hamiltonian (EH). The basic idea of EH theory is to obtain some selective eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian operator from the total eigenvalue spectrum. For this purpose, the entire configuration space is divided into a model and an outer space with projection operators \(P_{M}\) and \(Q_{M}\), respectively (see fig:ihfsmrcc_ms). The diagonalization of the EH takes care of the non-dynamic correlation coming from the interactions between the model space configurations. On the other hand, the dynamic correlation arises due to the interactions of the model space configurations with the outer space configurations. This interaction is introduced through a universal wave operator \(\Omega\), which is parametrized such that it generates the exact wave function when acting on the model space. The valence universal wave operator \(\Omega\) has the form

    \[ \begin{aligned} \Omega=e^{\tilde{S}^{\left( p,h \right)} }\end{aligned} \]

    where the braces indicate normal ordering of the cluster operators and \(\tilde{S}^{\left( p,h \right)}\) is defined as

    \[ \begin{aligned} \tilde{S}^{\left( p,h \right)}=\sum_{k=0}^{p}\sum_{l=0}^{h}\tilde{S}^{\left( k,l \right)}\end{aligned} \]

    The cluster operator \(\tilde{S}^{\left( k,l \right)}\) is capable of destroying exactly k active particles and l active holes, in addition to creation of holes and particles. The \(\tilde{S}^{\left( p,h \right)}\) subsumes all lower sector Fock space \(\tilde{S}^{\left( k,l \right)}\) operators. The \(\tilde{S}^{\left( 0,0 \right)}\) is equivalent to standard single-reference coupled cluster \(\hat{T}\) operator. The EH for (p,h) valence system can be defined as

    \[ \begin{aligned} \hat{H}_{eff}=P_{M}^{\left( p,h \right)}\Omega^{-1}\hat{H}\Omega P_{M}^{\left( p,h \right)}\end{aligned} \]

    However, \(\Omega^{-1}\) may not be well defined in all the cases. Therefore, the above definition for the EH is seldom used. Instead, the Block-Lindgren approach is generally used for solving the equations, which is defined by

    \[ \begin{aligned} P_{M}^{\left( p,h \right)}\left[ \hat{H}\Omega -\Omega\hat{H}_{eff}\right]P_{M}^{\left( p,h \right)}=0\end{aligned} \]
    \[ \begin{aligned} Q_{M}^{\left( p,h \right)}\left[ \hat{H}\Omega -\Omega\hat{H}_{eff}\right]P_{M}^{\left( p,h \right)}=0\end{aligned} \]

    Division of the configuration space into model and outer space in effective Hamiltonian (EH) theory and into model, intermediate, and outer space in intermediate Hamiltonian (IH) theory. \(P\) and \(Q\) denote the respective projection operators.

    When the model space is not energetically well separated from the outer space, this method faces convergence problems. This is commonly termed as the intruder state problem. In the intermediate Hamiltonian (IH) formulation, configuration space is divided into three subspaces, namely, the main(M), the intermediate(I), and the outer(O) space (see fig:ihfsmrcc_ms) with projection operators \(P_{M}\), \(P_{I}\) and \(Q_{O}\), respectively. The intermediate space acts as a buffer between the model and the outer space. When diagonalization the IH, a subset of the eigenvalues correspond to the main space obtained through EH theory. The IH is for the singly excited state sector (1,1) is defined as

    \[ \begin{aligned} H_{I}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}=P_{O}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}\bar{H}P_{O}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}+P_{O}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}\bar{H}Y^{\left( 1,1 \right)}P_{M}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}\end{aligned} \]

    where

    \[ \begin{aligned} Y^{\left( 1,1 \right)}=Q_{O}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}\left\{S_{2}^{\left( 0,1 \right)} +S_{2}^{\left( 1,0 \right)}+ S_{2}^{\left( 0,1 \right)}S_{1}^{\left( 1,0 \right)} +S_{2}^{\left( 1,0 \right)}S_{1}^{\left( 0,1 \right)}+S_{2}^{\left( 1,0 \right)}S_{2}^{\left( 0,1 \right)}\right\}P_{M}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}\end{aligned} \]

    The \(S^{\left( 1,0 \right)}\) and \(S^{\left( 0,1 \right)}\) are extracted from converged EOMIP-CCSD and EOMEA-CCSD calculations, respectively, by invoking intermediate normalization on the suitably chosen eigenvectors corresponding to active holes and active particles. The total procedure can be described as following

    • solve the ground state coupled cluster equations

    • construct \(\hat{\bar{H} }=e^{-\hat{T} }\hat{H}e^{\hat{T} }\)

    • solve the EOMIP and EOMEA equations

    • extract the \(\hat{S}\) amplitudes

    • construct the second similarity transformed Hamiltonian as \(H_{I}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}\)

    • diagonalize the \(H_{I}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}\) in CIS space

    The automatic active space selection scheme and all the speed up options which are available for STEOM-CCSD, including bt-PNO and COSX, are also available for IH-FSMR-CCSD. All the keywords controlling the IH-FSMR-CCSD are similar to STEOM-CCSD as described in spectroscopyproperties.steom.

    No UHF variant of IH-FSMR-CCSD is currently available.

    Properties¶

    The transition properties can be calculated using a simple CIS-like formulation, employing the converged IH-FSMR-CC eigenvectors. The transition moments are computed by default in an IH-FSMR-CCSD calculation.

    Solvation Correction¶

    Solvent effects can be approximated by a simple perturbative correction to the IH-FSMR-CCSD via

    \[ \begin{aligned} \omega_{k}=\omega^{0}_{k}+\frac{1}{2} \bar{V}^{\Delta}\bar{Q}^{\Delta}\end{aligned} \]

    where

    \[ \begin{aligned} \omega^{0}_{k}=\hat{L_{K} }H_{I}^{\left( 1,1 \right)} \hat{R_{K} }\end{aligned} \]

    The CPCM correction directly enters the \(H_{I}^{\left( 1,1 \right)}\), the modified Hatree-Fock orbitals. In the non-equilibrium regime, one can simply write the perturbative correction as

    \[ \begin{aligned} \omega_{k}^{neq}=\omega^{0,neq}_{k}+\frac{1}{2} \bar{V}\left( P_{\Delta}^{neq}\right) \bar{Q}\left( P_{\Delta}^{neq}\right)\end{aligned} \]

    where

    \[ \begin{aligned} P_{\Delta}^{neq}=L_{k}R_{k}\end{aligned} \]

    A typical input file looks like

    3
structure (0 1)
O        0.0000       0.0000       0.1173
H        0.0000       0.7572      -0.4692
H        0.0000      -0.7572      -0.4692
    ! aug-cc-pVDZ IH-FSMR-CCSD
!CPCM(water)
%mdci
NROOTS 8
DoSOLV true
DTol 1e-10
end
*xyz 0 1
O       0.0000  0.0000  0.1173
H       0.0000  0.7572 -0.4692
H       0.0000 -0.7572 -0.4692
*
